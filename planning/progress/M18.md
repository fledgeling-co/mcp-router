## What could not be drawn, and why

Three things the mock draws are not in the build, each measured rather than assumed.

1. **`path` ships as a refusal.** The router computes the search path it hands its children
   (`src/pool.ts:168`, `augmentPath`) and publishes it nowhere: `ControlAPIClient` declares
   nineteen methods and none returns a path or an environment. Reading *this app's* login-shell
   `PATH` instead would be a different list under the same heading — the app is a GUI process, the
   router is a launchd agent, and R6 exists because those differ. So the sheet is built, attached
   to the Settings window, and says what it will show and why it cannot yet. The first draft of the
   plan parked it outright; the out-of-family review called that an excuse against this repo's own
   two precedents (`SURF-010`'s pair sheet, `HeldVersionSheet`'s dimmed offers) and was right.
2. **The 30-day undo is not a thing this router does.** `ConfigWriter.backUp` keeps
   `backupsKept = 5` — five writes, not thirty days — and no control-API method reports a backup
   count, an age or a policy, so even "the last five are kept" would be quoting a constant the app
   cannot read. **Nothing was added**: the build never carried the claim, and this item did not
   introduce it. The gate table's "30-day undo stated on the surface" is therefore unmet by
   decision rather than by omission.
3. **`official` is drawn without the publisher grid.** `RegistryEntry` has no publisher field;
   `source` is which index supplied the row, not who published it. The fixture's names are
   `github`, `deepwiki` and `ai.smithery/Hint-Services-obsidian-github-mcp`, so a namespace parse
   would invent the ownership claim this sheet exists to disclaim. Grouping by `source` instead was
   rejected on this module's own note: a figure must come "never off `sources.official`, which is a
   pre-merge, pre-slice count of a different set." The omission is stated **on the surface**
   (`OfficialMarkCopy.publishersUnavailable`), not hidden.

### The blocker, reproduced before it was fixed, and the control kept

`.keyboardShortcut(.cancelAction)` sat on the destructive **Remove** button
(`Boards/CleanupSheets.swift:106` at `6721e5c`), so Escape performed an irreversible removal.
`planning/evidence/M18-gapfix-2/escape-shortcut-probe.swift` builds seven action-row shapes and
posts a keycode 53 or 36 at each, reporting which button's action ran. Committed rather than left in
`/tmp`, which is `G6`'s rule; reconstructed from the shape of the verifier's own probe rather than
recovered from it, and it adds the `Return` column and the candidate shapes.

| Shape | Cancel holds | Remove holds | Escape fires | Return fires |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pre-m18` | `.cancelAction` | — | **CANCEL** | **NEITHER** |
| `m18` | `.defaultAction` | `.cancelAction` | **REMOVE** | **CANCEL** |
| `stacked` | `.cancelAction` then `.defaultAction` | — | **CANCEL** | **NEITHER** |
| `stacked-rev` | `.defaultAction` then `.cancelAction` | — | **NEITHER** | **CANCEL** |
| `default-only` | `.defaultAction` | — | **NEITHER** | **CANCEL** |
| `exit-command` | `.defaultAction`, sheet carries `.onExitCommand` | — | **NEITHER** | **CANCEL** |
| `hidden-cancel` | `.defaultAction`, plus a hidden twin holding `.cancelAction` | — | **HIDDEN_CANCEL** | **CANCEL** |

`pre-m18` is the presence control: it proves a posted Escape reaches a `.cancelAction` button in
this harness, so `m18`'s `REMOVE` is an observation rather than a silence read as agreement. Row 2
also shows the two keys were **swapped** rather than one being lost — Return dismissed.

Four things that table settled, and three of them shaped the fix:

- **One control cannot hold both keys.** `stacked` and `stacked-rev` differ only in the order the
  two modifiers are written, and in each the *innermost* wins while the other fires nothing. No
  error, no warning. So "put `.cancelAction` on Cancel and keep `.defaultAction` there" was not
  available.
- **`.onExitCommand` on a sheet's own body is inert.** So a control carrying `.cancelAction` is the
  only route to Escape-dismissal on a macOS SwiftUI sheet — which is also why this defect is one
  token from correct: the only available route is the one that binds Escape to whichever button
  holds it.
- **The hidden-twin workaround does work**, and was declined on cost rather than capability. An
  invisible zero-size duplicate of the only control on an informational sheet buys one key and
  costs a control nobody can see. Recorded as measured-and-rejected so the next reader does not
  re-derive it.
- **A sheet that stays up blocks `NSApp.terminate`.** The first version of the probe hung on exactly
  the cells where nothing fired. That is an accidental second witness that the sheet really is still
  modal in a `NEITHER` cell, and it is why the probe calls `exit` instead.
