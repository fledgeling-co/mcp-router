Conduit — iOS surfaces

The phone is a triage tool, not a control surface. It finds things, judges them, and puts them in a queue that only the Mac can act on. Every screen is built around that one honest verb, which is also the security posture: a lost or unlocked phone still cannot install code on the laptop.

1 · Discover — the same two bands as the Mac, at thumb reach

Editorial and measured stay separated here too, and trending still carries its window and its sign. No row has an action on it: tapping opens the thing, and the only button in the app is on the detail screen. The rank column is the affordance that makes a ranked list scannable without turning it into a store shelf.

Question: what is worth a look? · Worst state: offline, which shows the last fetch with its age rather than an empty list.

2 · Triage — yes, no, later

The phone's own shape. Away from the Mac the only useful judgment is three-way, so the surface is a deck with three verbs rather than a store you browse. Swiping right stamps QUEUED, not INSTALLED, and the card says so — this is the screen where a dishonest verb would do the most damage. Every card carries the eval mark and the capability line before any marketing copy, so the decision is made on the two facts that matter.

Question: worth my Mac's attention, yes or no? · Worst state: the deck runs out, which is a finished job and reads like one.

3 · Queue and Library — what you sent, and what you already run

The queue is the phone's only writable state, so it shows a real status per item rather than a hopeful checkmark: waiting, installed, or declined at the Mac with the reason. The library is read-only on the phone — you can see what runs and where, and you cannot change it from here, which is stated rather than implied by greyed controls.

Question: did that land? · Worst state: declined at the Mac, which arrives with the reason attached.

4 · Pairing and Settings — the highest-stakes screen in the app

The pairing code grants a remote party a channel into the queue on someone's laptop. That is a bigger grant than a browser sync, so the two honesties Brave ships are the floor here: the warning is in plain language and names the consequence, and the expiry clock runs visibly while you are looking at it. The fallback ladder is scan → type a code → name the exact menu path on the Mac, because the third rung is what rescues a person whose camera fails.

Question: is this the right Mac, and is anyone else able to use this code? · Worst state: the code expires while you are reading it, which the ring shows before it happens.

Motion spec

Card swipe: rotation tracks the drag at 0.06°/px capped at 12°; the stamp fades in over the first 40pt of travel and is at full opacity by 90pt, so the verb is legible before the commit point rather than after it. Release below threshold springs back at response 0.3 / damping 0.75. Send-to-Mac: the card flies to the Queue tab and the tab badge increments as it arrives — one motion, one meaning, no separate toast. Queue status change: the pill cross-fades in place over 200ms; the row does not move, because a row that reorders under the thumb is a row you tap by accident. Pairing clock: the ring sweeps continuously and turns amber under 60s, red under 15s. Pull to refresh on Discover only; the other tabs have nothing to fetch. Under Reduce Motion the deck cross-fades between cards and the send animation becomes a badge increment with a haptic.
