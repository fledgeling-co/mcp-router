# Campaign run — 2026-08-20, full scope, test-campaign 0.8.0

**Scope: FULL.** Rung 1 — asked for, not inferred. Every case in the campaign ran.

**Verdict: accounting clears, the strict count does not reach 100%.** 44 of 52 cases checked
(85%), strict ratchet raised 33 → 36 → 43 → 44 over the run. 8 unchecked: 4 fail, 4 n/a, and **no
inconclusive and no blocked cases left**. `campaign.py check` reports every case accounted for and
every one of 20 surfaces carrying a case. Every capture is judged: witness 15 of 15, ratchet raised
5 → 12 → 15. Armed 44 of 44.

**`make all` is green** — `lint`, `build`, `test` (1473 tests in 179 suites), `test-ios` (36 tests,
2 skipped, 0 failures) and both parity stages — and `make test-ios-glass` is 5 of 5. It was red at
`test-ios` for most of this run; DEF-029 is what it was, and it is closed.

The three inconclusive cases and the ios-glass flake this report opened with are all closed, and
closing them is most of what the run did. Nine further defects were found afterwards by comparing
each iOS capture against a design reference that actually draws the surface — which had never been
done, because the references did not exist.

0.8.0 added a plane the campaign had never been measured on — whether a published picture
depicts what it is filed under — and the first run of it found this campaign guilty twice.

## What the new gate found here

`capture-lineage.py --gate` opened at **10 hard failures out of 10 published captures**: not one
had an entry in a capture manifest, so the only thing binding any picture to any surface was its
filename. Two of those filenames were lying, and the evidence to prove it had been sitting on
disk since the day the pictures were taken:

- `SURF-001.build.png` was byte-identical to `SURF-003.build.png`. The capture step photographed
  "whatever destination is restored" and filed it as the shell; the restored destination was
  Activity. The shell's picture was the Activity board.
- `SURF-010.build.png` was filed as the pairing sheet. `evidence/ax/SURF-010.title.txt` — written
  by the same script, in the same run, one line above the shutter — reads `Inbox`.

Both are DEF-010. Neither was visible to any gate that existed before this run.

## What changed

**The capture step now records what it was pointed at, as the shutter opens.** `capture-mac-glass.sh`
reads the window title back out of the running app and writes it as the capture's `target`; the tie
pass compares that against the surface's declared route. It is the readback, not the filename, that
can contradict the claim — and it did, immediately: the registry addressed a board as
`app://mac/evals` and the app answered `Checks` (**DEF-007**).

**The gate was watched to fail.** `--seed-swap` on seven different pairs: caught, 7 of 7.

**The reference half got the same treatment.** `mock/SURF-001.png` was byte-identical to
`mock/SURF-003.png` — the same shell-versus-Activity collision on the design side, which would have
handed a witness the wrong design of record. References are now deep-linked
(`?pane=…&sheet=…&only=…`), read their own state back after load, and record it. The three iOS
surfaces moved to the mock files that actually draw them, after the prototype reported a `pairing`
state change it did not render.

**Six iOS `n/a` cases were reachable all along.** They rested on a premise — "Simulator has no
accessibility tree, the app ships no URL scheme, activating Simulator.app is refused" — that is
true of the old lane and false of XCUITest, which runs out of process, reads iOS's own tree, and
never opens Simulator.app. The lane ran. Five iOS surfaces were photographed, each attributed to
its producing test by `xcresulttool` rather than by its filename.

**The phone has five tabs and the campaign had enumerated three.** Settings was photographed while
proving each tab renders its own surface — a surface nobody enumerated has no denominator, so no
case could ever have failed on it. Added as SURF-018 with two cases, one of which is red.

**Four leftover draft files were deleted.** `surfaces.json`, `requirements.json`, `flows.json` and
`components.json` carried the registry's ids bound to different content — `surfaces.json` called
SURF-013 "Mac add-server sheet" where the registry calls it "iOS Triage, Queue, Library", and 20 of
`requirements.json`'s ids carried a different requirement under the same number. `campaign.py add`
against any of them would have silently rebound every id. One capture had already been mis-filed
this way and is now named for what it shows.

## What the differential found

Vocabulary vector, controls only, shell and fixture rows subtracted:
`examined=8 boards · in-design-not-build=20 · in-build-not-design=16`.

The first attempt compared every word and returned 257 against 274 — a uniform flood across every
board, which is the signature of a dead predicate rather than a broken product. It was comparing an
app's terse control labels against a prototype's explanatory prose. Two instrument defects were
found and fixed before the numbers meant anything: `offsetParent` is 0 for all 39 of the
prototype's buttons under the sanctioned engine, so the visibility filter returned zero controls on
every board and read as "the design specifies nothing"; and `S.tab` defaults to `discover` and is
present on every load, so preferring it as the identity field reported "discover" for eight Mac
boards whose pictures were visibly different.

**DEF-011** is the finding worth acting on, and it is the one only this phase can see: Cleanup's
design gives every row trailing `Inspect` and `Remove` controls and the flagged row a `Read first…`;
the built board's rows carry no trailing controls at all. Confirmed by looking, not only by the
tree. Settings is the same shape — the design specifies `Pair another device` and `Unpair`, the
build draws neither, which is DEF-001 arriving through the design instead of through a failing
assertion. **DEF-012** records three renames where the design of record was not followed.

## The instrument that was off — DEF-029, closed

`make test-ios` was red at 52 failures across 35 tests when this run began, on an untouched `HEAD`
checkout as well as on the branch, and it is green at **36 tests, 2 skipped, 0 failures** now. The
cause was not this repo. The process-wide accessibility engine was off, so SwiftUI's
`_UIHostingView` vended an **empty element array** and every label assertion read empty and reported
`nothing was measured, so this proved nothing` — the honest verdict for a dead instrument, and
indistinguishable from inside the suite from a product that renders no copy.

The engine had been on by accident: something else on this machine had enabled it, which is why the
suite was green for months while depending on ambient host state no file in this repo controls.

The fix is in three parts, and the third is the one worth reading. Switching the engine on through
`_AXSSetAutomationEnabled` and reading `accessibilityElements` rather than
`accessibilityElementCount()` took the suite from 52 failures to 0 — and then `make all`, run
immediately afterwards on the same device, came back with **39**. The state is device-scoped and the
call lands on the *next* process: on a device left disabled, the run that re-enables it fails a
deterministic set of 15 test cases — identical set, measured three times — and the run after it,
unchanged, is clean. The 15 are exactly the ones hosting a `ScrollView`, and the tests that pass
alongside them are geometry, counts and negative assertions, so the green half of that run was
vacuous too. `test-ios` now runs a throwaway process before the graded one and discards its exit
code. Measured end to end: three runs with the call mutated to `(false)` drive the device into the
disabled state at 54 failures each, and the next run with the warm-up in place is 36 tests and 0
failures in 1.4 seconds.

Three arms, because everything else in that target rests on this one thing. Renaming the symbol
fails exactly the instrument check and nothing else, which proves only the resolution assertion.
Mutating the call to `(false)` on a device created for the arm reads **53 failures**, which is what
proves the other 35 tests depend on it. And the first run ever taken on a device whose engine had
never been live reads **0 failures**, which is why the warm-up is needed for an explicitly disabled
device and not for a fresh one. Both arm devices were deleted afterwards.

The instrument check is itself instrumented: it blocks on a probe hosting `ScrollView { Text }`,
read back through the same walk every other assertion uses, with a 20-second deadline. Two narrower
probes were built first and both were wrong in opposite directions — a bare `Text` reports the engine
live where 15 cases read empty, and a `ScrollView` read through the container path alone reports it
dead where 35 tests pass. A probe that answers differently from the thing it stands for answers a
different question.

Four hypotheses were refuted along the way — device contention, address recycling in the walker,
publication timing on a product surface, and machine saturation. The last was reported to the owner
as the cause before the probe ran, and it was wrong.
`X3-ios-unit-lane-empty-tree.md` carries the whole of it; the case is CASE-0126.

## The second pass — what a real design reference found

The first pass judged five capture pairs and left seven uncompared. Closing them exposed a
structural problem with the reference half that no gate had asked about: **the iOS references were
the top 1280x720 of a mock page**, because `obscura fetch --screenshot` has no viewport flag. On the
Mac prototype that is survivable. On the phone mocks it is not — Queue lives 4205px down
`i3-phone-triage.html` and Library 5912px — so the reference for either named a subject it did not
show, which is the hazard `capture-lineage` exists to close, arriving on the design side.

`planning/test-campaign/bin/capture-phone-mock.sh` fixes it: it removes every unit but the named
one, moves it to the document top, reads the unit's caption back out of the page, and **fails rather
than capturing the whole page** when the named unit is not found. Five references are now
unit-scoped and record which section and unit they show.

The first thing that bought was a correction to this campaign's own record. **SURF-018 was judged
`inconclusive` on a false premise** — "iOS Settings has no design of record". It has one, in
`i1-phone-pairing.html` §B, §I, §J and §L. The earlier pass compared the phone's Settings against
`prototype.html`, which is the Mac console, found nothing, and recorded the absence as the design's
rather than as its own.

Comparing the real halves found nine defects, six of them closed:

| | |
|---|---|
| **DEF-024** closed | Every pairing action drew at label width where the design draws it at content width. Eleven call sites declared `.frame(maxWidth: .infinity)` on the `Button` — outside the style — so the layout frame stretched while the style's background wrapped a hugging label. `PhoneButtonStyle.swift`'s own doc comment already records this mistake for *height*; it arrived again through width. No assertion could see it, because the frame was already full width. Guarded now by a painted-band oracle rather than a frame read, armed at 148pt inside a 393pt container. |
| **DEF-026** closed | The never-paired Settings surface stated the narrowing twice — once from the state's own block, once from an About section that renders it unconditionally. §B states it once. |
| **DEF-027** closed | The camera pre-prompt was toned neutral where `.wants` is `--attn` throughout. `PhoneMessageBlock.Tone.caution` carried the design's own rationale in its doc comment and this surface was the one place it was earned and unused. |
| **DEF-021/022/023** closed earlier in the run | The typed-entry field, its headline, and the pairing route's name. |
| **DEF-025** open | The design draws the Settings recovery action below its notice card; the build renders it as the card's last child. Moving it changes the block's contract for all nine states. |
| **DEF-028** open | §B draws never-paired as a centred empty state with a Mac glyph; the build draws a left-aligned notice. The same block draws eight other states the design does not centre. |

Two more corrections came out of the same pass and are worth naming because both were the campaign
lying quietly rather than the product failing. **A capture filed as `settings-paired-state` showed
the never-paired state**; it is renamed at the shutter, not in the manifest. And **CASE-0121 spoke
for three surfaces at once** — the same understatement the tie pass found in the registry, arriving
in the case file. It is now CASE-0121, CASE-0124 and CASE-0125, each with its own capture and each
armed.

**The per-surface assertions were strengthened before those cases were armed.** Each of the three
asserted only that it was *not* Settings, and a not-that predicate is satisfied by every other
screen in the app, including a blank one — which is the shape a missing body actually takes. Each
now names a string only it renders.

## What this run did not do

- **`export-warrant` was run and its output removed.** This repository carries no `.warrant/`, so
  the export wrote two files nothing reads. Recorded rather than left in the tree.
- **No product code was changed to make a gate pass.** One change was made to the ios-glass lane's
  own test file, on `ai/x2`: the pairing test now photographs the Settings surface after
  `selectTab` has proved which surface it is and before the state assertion that has been failing,
  so a red run yields a picture. It did not fix the flake and was not meant to.
- **`ai/x2` is not merged.** The iOS lane's artifact is built from that branch and the lane proof
  says so. DEF-X2-a and DEF-X2-b are both closed (they became DEF-013 and DEF-017), so the reasons
  that branch was held back are gone; whether it lands in `main` is still the owner's call.
- **`make test-ios-glass` is still not in `make all`.** `X2-ios-on-glass.md` says it joins when
  DEF-X2-b and DEF-X2-c close, and both have; the lane is 5 of 5 on eight separate runs now. It is
  left out of this pass because wiring it is a change to what every commit pays for — roughly two
  minutes and a booted simulator — and that is a cost decision rather than a stability one. Put to
  the owner.

- **DEF-008 was attempted and reverted, which is why it is still open.** `.accessibilityIdentifier`
  on a tab item **clears** the button's identifier instead of setting it, measured on glass in both
  places SwiftUI offers. The finding is recorded in `PhoneShell.swift` beside the label so the next
  person does not spend the same two runs.

- **DEF-030 is new and is about this suite rather than the product.** Four async package tests fail
  at a host load average near 700 and pass at 46, on the same source minutes apart, and the failing
  set is not stable between runs. They assert on a deadline the machine can miss, which is how a
  lane failure comes to be read as a product failure — the mistake DEF-029 cost ten runs.
