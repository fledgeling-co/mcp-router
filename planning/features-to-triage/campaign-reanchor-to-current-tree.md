# Re-anchor the test campaign to the tree it is meant to describe

- audience: Whoever needs the remaining-work figure to mean something
- platforms: n/a
- proposed-by-ai: false

## What and why
The campaign's evidence was recorded against the project as it stood on 24 August, and the project has
moved substantially since. Every figure the campaign publishes — cases passing, requirements observed,
surfaces covered — therefore describes a version of the product that no longer exists, and it does so
without saying that anywhere a reader would see. Because the remaining-work reckoning is built by
reconciling briefs against that campaign, the reckoning inherits the same blind spot: a full day of
completed and merged work left its totals unchanged.

The cost is not that the numbers are wrong in detail. It is that a reader cannot tell a figure that was
re-measured from one that was merely carried, so an unchanged total reads as "no progress" when it may
equally mean "nobody looked again".

## Acceptance sketch
- The campaign records which version of the project each result was measured against.
- Re-running the campaign against the current project updates its figures, and results carried from an
  earlier run are visibly marked as carried rather than re-measured.
- Work merged since the last run either gains its own coverage or is listed as not yet covered.
- The published evidence page states how old its evidence is, in a place a reader meets before the totals.
- The remaining-work reckoning states the age of the evidence it rests on.

## Assumptions made writing this
- Assuming the remedy is to re-measure and to date every figure, rather than to re-record old results
  against the new version, which would carry the staleness forward invisibly.
- Assuming a carried result is legitimate when it is labelled as carried, rather than requiring every
  figure to be re-measured on every run.
