# Adjudication — 2026-08-22

The two steps the script stops before, done by hand. Both changed the answer.

## The join is structurally broken in this repository, and 53 of the 75 `unbuilt` rows are merged

19.4% is not a bad join; it is the wrong join. **This pipeline tracks item status in
`planning/features-to-triage/LEDGER.md` by item id — `M15`, `R19`, `G4` — and not by campaign id.**
Briefs cite `CASE-*` and `DEF-*` only when a defect prompted them, which is 18 of 93. The other 75
are unjoinable by construction, and `unbuilt` for them means *not tied to the registry*, not *not
built*.

Crossed against the project's own status column:

| The 75 `unbuilt` briefs say | Count |
|---|---:|
| **Merged** | **49** |
| Done | 4 |
| Ready to verify | 5 |
| Ready for AI | 3 |
| To Do | 8 |
| Needs More Work | 3 |
| Retired | 1 |
| Not in the ledger at all | 1 (`BLOCKED-apple-identity`) |
| Other | 1 |

**53 are merged or done** — built, and gated by merged-tree runs the orchestrator re-ran rather
than took on report. `F1` (the Swift workspace), `M1` (the Mac shell), `I2` (iPhone Discover) are
in that list. Calling them unbuilt is false, and the headline's 149 product-work items overstates
by roughly that many.

What is genuinely outstanding among the 75 is about **nineteen**: 8 To Do, 3 Ready for AI, 3 Needs
More Work, 5 in flight awaiting a verdict.

**This is worth fixing rather than re-adjudicating every run.** Either briefs carry the campaign
ids they answer to, or `reckon` learns to read a repo-declared status column. Until one of those,
every reckoning here will report the backlog as unbuilt.

## `broken` is 57 defects, of which thirteen are genuinely open

The inventory's own status field: 21 `closed`, 15 `fixed-in-campaign`, 3 `fixed-in-test`, 1 `fixed`,
1 `fixed-in-part`, 1 `fixed-upstream`, 1 `mitigated-in-campaign`, 1 `superseded-by-DEF-055`. That
leaves **5 `open-detector`, 3 `open`, 3 `open-measured`, 2 `open-owner` — thirteen**. `broken` here
is reading *a defect exists* rather than *a defect is open*, which is the same shape as the join
problem one column over.

## What survives adjudication, and it is the part worth acting on

**`requirements_observed` — 11 of 25 — is untouched by any of the above**, because requirements
join to cases directly and never went through the brief join. **Fourteen of twenty-five stated
requirements have never been watched happen.** Twelve are `unmeasured` and two are `undecided`
(`REQ-016`, a pairing exchange actually contacting the other device; `REQ-019`, a refused write
shown as the server's refusal).

That is the honest headline of this reckoning and it is not what the generated one says:

> **The product's code is in far better shape than its evidence. 95% of designed cases reached a
> verdict and 96% of surfaces are spoken for — but 56% of what the project says it does has never
> been observed, only reported by the project about itself.**

`REQ-007` is the sharpest of the twelve: *numbers the router does not observe are never displayed*.
That is the honesty guarantee the whole product rests on, it is stated as a requirement, and
nothing has ever watched it hold.

## What to do first

**One.** Fix the join, in the pipeline rather than in the reckoning — it is a one-line convention
(briefs name the requirement or case they answer to) and it makes every future run of this skill
worth reading. Left alone it will report ~50 merged items as unbuilt every time, and a report that
is wrong in a known direction stops being read.

**Two.** Take the twelve `unmeasured` requirements as evidence-work, not feature-work. They belong
to whoever owns the harness, and each needs its own remedy — a hook to force the state, an
instrument that can read the answer, or a decision about what a pass would look like. `REQ-007`
goes first because it is the guarantee the product's credibility rests on.

**Three.** The two `undecided` rows are the owner's, and they are cheap: both are cases where the
documents and the evidence disagree rather than where work is missing.

## What this reckoning cannot speak for

The campaign registry was read from a **working tree another session is actively editing** — 16
tracked files under `planning/test-campaign/` carry uncommitted modifications. The figures are of
that state, not of a committed one, and a re-run after that session lands may differ. Nothing here
was written into that directory.

`briefs_joined` at 19.4% means the retirement claims this skill would normally make are withheld by
its own gate, and correctly so. Nothing above promotes an item to done on the strength of a ledger
status: 53 rows are demoted from *unbuilt* to *the project says it is merged*, which is a weaker
claim than campaign evidence and is stated as one.
