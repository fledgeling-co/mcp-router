`strict-check.py` reported CHECKED 91 of 114, and named 12 of the 23 unchecked as
*only proves something rendered*. That bucket was the item: 7 failures belong to whoever owns
those defects and 4 n/a are recorded limits, so the twelve were the only way the count could move
without a defect being fixed first.

The twelve were not one kind of case. Reading each one against the instrument it actually runs
splits them four ways, and two of the four are a raise:

| | cases | what changed |
|---|---|---|
| Read one window, could read across several | CASE-0131, CASE-0167, CASE-0176 | raised to `metamorphic` on new relations in `bin/glass-assert.py`, four arms |
| Proved a heading exists, could tie the frame to the wire and to its own pixels | CASE-0188, CASE-0189 | raised to `metamorphic` on four new checks in `bin/g17_document_assert.py`, one plant, four named watchers |

| Its subject is not a product state | CASE-0194 | stays `structural`, with the reason written as permanent |

`strict-check.py`: **91 → 96 of 114 (80% → 84%)**, ratchet raised to 96 in the same commit.
The bucket falls from 12 to 7.

## The first raise: three relations, and why a relation is the right shape

Every predicate in `glass-assert.py` read **one** window. That settles copy and it cannot settle a
control: an `AXButton` with the right label is in the tree whether or not its handler does
anything, which is exactly what CASE-0167 and CASE-0176 said about themselves —
*PROVED TO RENDER, NOT PROVED TO WORK, and recorded at `structural` for that reason*.

The obvious raise is to press the control. It is also the wrong one here, and CASE-0167's own note
says why: the Debug shell reads a fixture, so a second read returns the same bytes and a green
"it refreshed" would be indistinguishable from a button wired to nothing. Pressing would have
bought a stronger-looking rung over a weaker claim.

So `RELATIONS` reads a board **across its readings** instead and requires the drawn control set to
move the way the data moved. What that catches is a board drawing a fixed column, which no
single-window presence check can see.

| relation | span | measured |
|---|---|---|
| `SURF-007/row-actions` | 2 windows | 3 rows and 5 rows; 8 of 8 own their own `AXButton`s one level below their group; `[Inspect, Remove…]` on the 7 rows with no provenance note, `[Read first…]` on the 1 that has one |
| `SURF-025/controls-track-reading` | 3 readings | `Rescan` enabled and `Reconcile all…` disabled in all three; per-harness controls 10 / 0 / 0 |
| `SURF-026/action-tracks-state` | 3 readings | exactly one board action each — `Analyse now` (disabled) / `Watch the live log instead` / `Start the router` — pairwise distinct, none appearing in another reading's window |

`SURF-007/row-actions` is the one that replaces a source read with a **stronger** instrument rather
than a differently-shaped one. `CleanupRowActionsTests` asserts that `CleanupBoardRow` declares
`accessibilityElement(children: .contain)` and not `.combine`. That is a proxy: what the modifier
decides is whether each row's buttons are separately addressable, and combining flattens them into
the row's own label so the group survives and its controls do not. The relation reads that
property directly, off two dumps of a running window.

### The arms, and why one relation needed two

An arm is a substitution rather than a deletion: one reading's window is read out of another
reading's file, which is the regression the relation exists to catch. Each arm reddens its own
case and nothing else — the 22 standing failures on this suite are SURF-001's and SURF-011's, and
they are identical before and after.

| arm | plants | reddens |
|---|---|---|
| `SURF-007/fixed-column` | the skills window read from the servers window | the count half and the flagged-row half |
| `SURF-007/combined-rows` | the buttons a `.combine` row stops publishing, removed — 6 from one dump, 9 from the other, named in the run | ownership: `0/3` and `0/5` |
| `SURF-025/rows-through-empty` | the empty window read from the populated file | per-harness count reads 10 where it must read 0 |
| `SURF-026/one-fixed-action` | the empty window read from the populated file | expected-action, pairwise-distinct and no-stray-action |

`SURF-007` needed both. A substitution reddens the count and leaves the ownership half — the half
the case is actually about — unwatched, and an arm that watches one of two halves is an arm that
has measured one of them.

The plant names what it removed rather than removing it silently: an arm whose predicate matched
nothing removes nothing and leaves the relation green, which from outside is indistinguishable
from a relation that does not bite. `relation_dump` records every dropped control and prints it,
which is also what `reader-accounting.py` requires of it — the gate found the drop on the first
run and it is recorded rather than declared, so `reader-accounting.tsv` is unchanged at 39 rows.

## The second raise: two tab steps that stood on a heading

CASE-0188 and CASE-0189 are FLOW-006.03 and .04 — the Changelog and Capabilities tabs of the
capability-document panel. What each asserted was that a heading carrying the tab's own name is in
the frame's measurement dump, which is true of a panel that draws a heading. Their own notes said
so: *At `structural-visual`, so it moves no count.*

Four checks replace that, two per case, and neither is a stronger-sounding restatement of the old
one:

| check | asks | measured |
|---|---|---|
| `G17-P6W` / `G17-P7W` | is what the panel drew the document THE ROUTER SERVED? | 4 of 4 words the wire response carries only in that document are in the panel's own body; 0 of the 20 unique to the read me are |
| `G17-P6R` / `G17-P7R` | did the tab REPAINT? | 22.7% and 21.8% of a 1,572,864-pixel body region differ from the read me frame's, against a 1% floor |

The discriminating vocabulary is **computed from `wire-served.json`**, not written into the check,
so it cannot be satisfied by a string the assertion file happens to know; and both denominators are
checked before the result, because a wire response whose three documents shared every word would
give both lists empty and hand a clean answer to a search that examined nothing. The raster half
crops each frame's body region from that frame's **own** dump at its own scale, so the titlebar and
the tab bar — which should be nearly identical across the three — cannot supply the difference.

Rated `metamorphic` rather than `raster-visual`, which was the one real fork here. Both are effect
rungs and the count is the same either way, so the choice is about which word is true: the claim is
a relation (vary the tab, require the body to track the document the router served) rather than a
comparison against a reference image, and `MeasureDump` renders off the hosting view's own backing
store rather than off a display server, which is what `raster-visual` names. Referred to
`claude-fable-5` at high effort with the definitions, both candidates and the consequences of
each; it returned `metamorphic` on the same two grounds and named no better option. The
`raster-visual` reading would also have obliged an `inspect_raster` sweep over every evidence path
on the case, adding six rows to a 28-row standing blocker for artifacts that were never claimed to
be pictures.

### One plant, four named watchers

The arm is control `G17-P8`'s: the changelog and capabilities frames are re-rendered on the read me
tab under their own filenames. `G17-P6W` goes red naming what is missing and what strayed,
`G17-P6R` reads 0.0% against its 1% floor, and the same pair bites for `.04`. Each is named
separately — `armed/tabs-served-changelog.txt`, `armed/tabs-raster-changelog.txt` and their two
siblings — so a tab step that stopped biting is visible as itself rather than only as CASE-0190's
relation over all three frames. The four files agree line for line because they are one plant's
record filed under each check watched through it, which is stated in the cases rather than left to
be discovered.

Both frames were restored and re-rendered **byte-identically** against the shas the manifest already
held: `5563977a…` for the changelog and `3c5aa28b…` for the capability list.

## The six that read source, and the rung that already existed for them

Six of the twelve run no product code at all. They read Swift source, comment-stripped, and their
own notes said so — *both read source text, and no assertion here proves a header rendered*. They
were rated `structural-visual`, which is a rung **on** the product ladder, one step below an
effect. That is what made them look movable, and it is the wrong reading twice over: they are not
one rung below an effect, they are off the axis, and the registry has had `source-analysis` for
exactly this since DEF-057.

Re-rating them moves no number — `source-analysis` buys no effect credit and the strict count is
unchanged by it. What it buys is that the next reader of this bucket does not spend the time again,
and that `campaign.py` now holds each of them to naming its analyzer and the denominator it ran
over, because *the grep found nothing* is the reading a grep gives when it is pointed at the wrong
file.

| case | analyzer reads | units | the rendered claim, where it exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| CASE-0128 | `ActivityBoard.swift`'s `header` slice, `CleanupPresentation.resetLabel` | 2 | CASE-0011 (`outcome`, macos-glass) asserts `Reset history…` on the running Activity window |
| CASE-0129 | the constant, `SkillsBoard.swift`, `MenuCommand.swift` | 3 | CASE-0012 (`outcome`, macos-glass) asserts `Add marketplace…` |
| CASE-0136 | the `CleanupBoardRow` declaration | 1 | CASE-0138 (`outcome`, macos-glass), and CASE-0131's relation |
| CASE-0137 | the `SkillProvenanceSheet` declaration | 1 | CASE-0140 (`outcome`, macos-glass) presses `Read first…` |
| CASE-0149 | 3 declarations in `PairedMacSettingsView.swift` | 3 | none — owed on ios-glass |
| CASE-0150 | 2 declarations in `PairedMacSettingsView.swift` | 2 | none — owed on ios-glass |

**CASE-0129 is permanent and the others are not.** Its load-bearing half is that neither
`SkillsBoard.swift` nor `MenuCommand.swift` writes the marketplaces label as a literal any more,
and a literal and a constant *render identically* — same pixels, same accessibility node. There is
no product-side observable, so no rung on the ladder can ever carry it.

**CASE-0149 and CASE-0150 are owed rather than permanent**, and their own arms record already said
so: *That the rendered control sits below the card in pixels, or that the glyph rasterises and the
column reads as centred on a device* is named there as outstanding and wanting a fresh on-glass
capture. REQ-005 rests on these two cases alone, so it rests entirely on source analysis today.
The campaign's `covered_source_only` guard does not hold the gate on it because REQ-005 claims no
effect outside the process — which is correct and is also the reason nothing else was going to
report this.

## CASE-0194, permanently structural

Its subject is the correspondence between the capture manifest and the artifacts on disk. That is
a property of the evidence record rather than of the product, so there is no product state a change
could move and no rung it could climb to. It stays in the denominator: a campaign that hid its own
record-keeping checks from the total would be scoring itself on a smaller suite than it runs.

## What did not land

**CASE-0149 and CASE-0150 are still unraised, and they are owed rather than closed.** Raising them
is not a re-rating: it needs a new instrument — a placement assertion over a frame of the iOS
Settings surface, proving the recovery action sits below the message card in pixels and that the
never-paired column reads as centred. That wants the `ios-glass` lane (an iOS build, a booted
simulator and an XCUITest runner attached out-of-process), and this machine was at 0% idle with
119 GB of 128 GB in use for the whole of this session. Renting the claim to the `swiftui` lane
instead was considered and rejected: `PairedMacSettingsView.swift` does live in `MCPRouterUI`, so
`MeasureDump` could host it, but a phone view hosted on macOS is not the device the case is about,
and a frame taken that way would raise the rung while lowering what the case knows.

## A build failure worth recording

`swift build --product MeasureDump` fails in this worktree with ~10 `Operation not permitted`
errors on `.d`, `.dia` and index-store writes under `app/.build`, while the same paths are
writable from the shell in the same second. It is **SwiftPM's own sandbox**, not the filesystem and
not the harness: `swift build --disable-sandbox` completes in 50 s with 0 such errors. Nothing in
the message names a sandbox, and the failing writes are the compiler's side outputs rather than its
products, so a partial `.build` is left behind and the next run reports a different subset — which
reads as flakiness. `scripts/worktree-preflight.sh` clears the run first and passes, so this is a
third way the two spellings of a worktree path can end a build, and it is not the two the preflight
already refuses.
